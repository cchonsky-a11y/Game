# SYSTEMS.md — Canonical Rules

> The authoritative rules and numbers. When this file and the GDD disagree, this file wins; update both.
> Describing a system here does **not** mean it is in current scope. See PROTOTYPE_SCOPE.md.
> Values marked (tune) are starting points to be validated in prototypes.

## 1. Determinism and causality
- The simulation is deterministic: the same seed and the same player inputs always produce the same result.
- All randomness comes from a seeded generator owned by the simulation.
- **Event log.** Every meaningful state change is recorded as an event with:
  - `id`, `time`, `type`, `target`
  - `immediateCauses` (event ids)
  - `actors` (who acted, including the player)
  - `effects` (state deltas)
- Current conditions during an era must be explainable from the log ("Why?"). Multi-century causal ancestry and post-jump causal chains are **not** required.
- The language model never decides outcomes; it only describes logged events. Generated text is cached in the save.

## 2. Time
- Game time is measured in years. All simulated change (aging, debt, decay, drift, AI actions) scales per year, never per turn.
- Turn length by stage: 1 = 1 month; 2 = 2 months; 3 = 3 months; 4 = 6 months; 5 = 1 year.
- The player may shorten turn length at any time, never lengthen it beyond the stage cap.
- **P1 (decided 2026-10-02, Corey):** in the Rome era a turn is **1 calendar month**, fixed, with no player-facing turn-length choice; durations are authored in months (machine assessment 2 months). Spending the last Attention never ends the month: the player ends it explicitly (**End Month**), and an opt-in fast-forward stops on meaningful interruptions. The stage table above applies to later eras.
- **P1 scene pacing (Corey, locked 2026-10-02; implemented 2026-10-04).** Eight scene categories (Engineering, Personal, RomanLife, WorkEconomy, MachineMystery, CityHistory, Exploration, InstitutionsPolitics). Each month every system offers its optional meaningful scenes as candidates (a commission's encounter, an inviter's next invitation, a Grand Challenge whose question the work raised, a person's offscreen development, authored Roman-life and machine-mystery scenes) and one router picks at most one (`scenes.optionalPerMonth`, P1_PROPOSALS P1-11). A category that has had two scenes in a row is left out while any other category could happen; only when nothing else can is it allowed, at the deprioritized weight (`scenes.repeatedCategoryWeight`). The player can stay focused on a category (`focus`), which lifts this for it. World interruptions (illness, fire, Rome's dated events, the plague, critical authored moments such as R-17 ACTIVE) and the player's own actions are never routed or delayed; they count for pacing. A candidate not picked waits for a later month. **Revised 2026-10-04:** candidates that move the game on (commissions, invitations, Grand Challenges, people's lives) weigh 3 (`scenes.progressionWeight`), authored texture 1, so a richer Rome doesn't starve the plot (P1-11). **Roman-life texture can recur:** a reusable scene comes back after its cooldown with its next variant, never the same words twice, and is done when its variants run out; scenes can cost money and can put a choice (a vow at Fortuna Redux before the jump).
- Jumps are simulated in decade steps (coarse mode) using the same rules.
- Undo is allowed within a turn, except for actions that reveal information (experiments, conversations, investigations).

## 3. The inventor
- **Attention:** 4 per turn (P1: per month); −1 when old or unwell; +1 with an apprentice or secretary. Never scales with stage. Can be borrowed from the next turn during a crisis at a health cost.
- **P1 (Corey, implemented 2026-10-04): no silent overbooking.** Multi-month work (projects, commissions, Grand Challenge stages, machine work, inventions, mentoring) reserves its Attention in each later month it runs, and an office reserves its duties every month. Before any of it starts, every later month it would touch is checked: if what is already pledged for that month plus the new work exceeds the 4, it is refused with the month and the numbers ("That would reserve 1 Attention next month, but 4 of your 4 are already committed then."). Zero free Attention is legal; only End Month moves time. The console takes one numbered choice at a time.
- **Healthy years** (expected maximum age with the best available care): baseline ~70; well-advanced medicine ~85; heavily advanced ~95. Mortality risk rises near the ceiling; the ceiling is predictable.
- Starts at age 28. Ages only within eras, never during jumps.
- **Succession:** a prepared successor inherits the machine; knowledge transfers through the Journal. No successor: Standard mode restarts from the latest checkpoint; Ironman ends the run.

## 4. Absorption
Every idea has three bars: **Knowledge**, **Means**, **Carriers** (0–100).
- Adoption speed is driven by the minimum bar.
- Adoption accuracy is driven by the mean of the bars.
- Low accuracy produces **distorted adoption**: an altered variant with partial benefits and side effects.
- Applies to technology, institutions, administration, finance, and social reforms.

## 5. Care domains, priorities, and projects
- Domains: Agriculture, Medicine, Knowledge, Governance, Military, Economy, Infrastructure, Faith and Culture.
- Each region has a level per domain. Levels decay without upkeep.
- The player sets each domain per region to **Protect**, **Maintain**, or **Accept Risk**, but **only for a domain where they have a voice (a 25% stake, §7) in an institution that maintains it**; their institutions there pay the upkeep, split by priority and scaled by their sway (§7). A domain they have no voice in **follows Rome's real history** (the historical curve), at no cost to them; only their projects and the consequences of their choices move it off that curve. With a voice, priorities act **relative to history**: Protect +2 a year, Maintain ±0, Accept Risk −8 (tune), multiplied by the player's **sway** over the domain (§7). The inventor starts with no responsibilities: they survive on work, and work income is taxed (P0: 10%, tune).
- Investment happens through **projects** (templated per domain, re-dressed per era) that change domain levels. The player never sets a domain level directly. **Authority (decided 2026-09-28):** a public project (law, administration, public works, markets, coinage) needs influence (10%) in an institution of its domain to be pushed through; a private one (a repair, a physician, training) needs none. Measures against a historical crisis need 10% of a relevant institution, but once the crisis's first warning proves the inventor's foreknowledge right, membership is enough.

## 6. Expectations, debt, and crises
- **Expectation** = max(historical benchmark for the era, the region's recent peak fading over time). (tune; split into social and physical values if P0 shows players avoiding improvement)
- **Debt accrual per year** = max(0, expectation − level) × rate (rate = 1, tune).
- **Compounding:** existing debt grows 5% per year. During a jump, compounding stops **30 years after departure**. After those 30 years, a domain that no surviving institution maintains **stops accruing new debt** and drifts toward its long-run target (see §8); maintained domains keep the accrual rule.
- **Tiers:** Stable → Strained → Fragile → Critical; each raises the yearly crisis chance. Severity scales with debt.
- **History is the baseline (decided 2026-09-28).** Everything, in every domain, follows what happened historically unless the player does something that directly or indirectly changes it. Every departure from history is caused by the player's actions and logged as such.
- **Plague (the Medicine crisis):** a historical crisis comes on its **historical dates**; the player changes **how hard it hits, not when** (P0: the Antonine plague's warnings in AD 165–166, the outbreak in late AD 166). Left alone, it strikes as history had it: the historical death toll (P0: about 10% of Rome) and each domain's historical drop. Severity = the historical severity × (Hazard ÷ historical Hazard) × ((1 − Resilience) ÷ (1 − historical Resilience)); level damage = the historical drop × severity ÷ historical severity. Medicine debt raises Hazard; Governance and Economy debt tiers raise severity: × (1 + Governance tier bonus + Economy tier bonus), with a bonus of 0 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.35 for Stable / Strained / Fragile / Critical (tune). A domain following history takes the plague's step once, from the event, not again from the historical trend. Recurrences: random recurrences are off (decided 2026-09-28: they fell on no historical date and broke history as the baseline). **The historical ones come on their dates (built 2026-09-28):** the plague in Rome under Commodus (AD 189; P0: 3% of Rome as history had it) and the Plague of Cyprian (about AD 251; 8%), each a step in the history curve (0.3 and 0.8 × the Antonine drop). Left alone they strike as history had it; hazard × (1 − resilience) against history's (with the debt-tier multiplier) scales the deaths and the part of the drop that differs from history, which is the player's mark (tune).
- **Warning stages:** every major crisis passes through visible antecedent stages before breaking out.
- **Paying down debt** costs 1.5× the gold that prevention would have cost.
- A crisis releases (clears) debt, resets expectations to current levels, and damages levels, population, and wealth. In the medium term (about 10 years) a plague must always leave the Index lower than no plague would; if it doesn't, crisis damage is raised until the levels lost outweigh the debt cleared. The outbreak ends with: "The sickness burns itself out. Nothing is fixed; there are simply fewer people left, and the survivors expect less."
- **Plague immunity:** no new plague outbreak within **30 years** of the last one, except the historical recurrences, which come on their dates (decided 2026-09-28).
- **Plague severity labels** follow the share of the region's population that dies: **Contained** (under 5%), **Severe** (5–15%), **Catastrophic** (over 15%). The death toll is always shown.
- **Crisis outcomes branch:** crises can also create openings (migration, orphaned talent, new movements, experimentation, scattered scholars).
- **Boom and bust (the Economy crisis):** interventionist economic policy builds **malinvestment** (P0: 8 a year per interventionist stance). Once it reaches 20 the first of **three visible warnings** appears (prices outrun wages; moneychangers refuse new coin; credit dries up), advancing at most one a year with chance malinvestment × 0.01 (max 0.9). The bust liquidates it: Economy damage 0.3 × malinvestment, Governance 0.1 ×, a quarter of the inventor's gold lost, Economy debt cleared and expectations reset. (tune)
- **Spillover:** debt spreads along trade routes, borders, and shared institutions in proportion to connection strength.

## 7. Institutions
- **P1 (decided 2026-10-02, Corey): relationship-first access replaces stake buying.** Access goes aware → knows a member → guest → invited back → sponsored candidate → member → office/leadership. A forward invitation needs a specific inviter, an existing relationship, relevant work in that domain, demonstrated usefulness, and an inviter willing to take the social risk; fame alone never qualifies. Institutions create obligations (dues, meals, mutual aid, funeral contributions, disputes, standards, arbitration). The stake rules below are the P0 model; they are retired institution by institution as each migrates (P1_PROPOSALS P1-03). **Migrated so far: the Merchants' Guild of Ostia** (2026-10-03): its seats aren't for sale; Felix's referral after real work leads to a guest supper, an invitation back, his sponsorship and the members' vote; admission costs the entry fee and brings dues and the guild's obligations; a member holds the standing P0 systems read at 10%. **And the Physicians' Circle** (2026-10-04), through the physician Serenus. **The gate's evidence is kept apart (implemented 2026-10-04):** relationship, relevant work and demonstrated usefulness each have their own requirements in content (for the Circle: knowing Serenus; medicine work or the promise; Serenus's regard of 2). **Admission is a vote, not a timer:** the members vote the candidate in with a chance of 0.8, +0.05 per point of the sponsor's regard, −0.3 if the institution holds a grievance (P1_PROPOSALS P1-13); a failed vote is put off once, and a second failure is a refusal (back to "invited back", the sponsor waits). An affordable stake purchase no longer stops fast-forward. **Access kinds (implemented 2026-10-04):** the two Senate factions don't sell places; a senator's following takes clients through a patron's introduction, which isn't built yet, so they take no new members in P1 play (OPEN DESIGN QUESTION); the Tiber Island sanctuary takes gifts (`give`) and names its givers benefactors, without shares; the banking house keeps real financial stakes. Rome's choices now win **standing** (members' leaders think better of you; a house you don't belong to takes an interest), never shares, except at the bank. LEGACY COMPATIBILITY: the internal stake still records standing for P0 systems (offices, voice, policy) and the batch/explorer/snapshot still use the old purchase. (The P0 batch strategies and explorer still use the simulation's legacy purchase for regression until they are migrated.)
- Attributes: `type` (political, religious, commercial, scholarly, military), `reach`, `capacity` (directives per turn), `loyalty`, `leader` (a named notable with a successor), `identity`, `drift`.
- Directive effectiveness = capacity × loyalty, within reach, limited by type.
- **Identities:** charitable, commercial, bureaucratic, militant, ritualistic, isolationist, reformist, politically powerful. Drift moves identity toward the institution's own interests; charters, founding principles, and reverence for the founder slow it.
- **Decay per decade during absence:** bare 10%; chartered and endowed 3%; chartered, endowed, in a thriving region, keeping the Legend alive 1%.
- **Outcomes on arrival:** Thriving, Drifted, Captured, Dissolved, Rogue. Rogue institutions become AI actors.
- Institutions maintain the domains matching their type while the inventor is away: strength × rate × control factor × min(1, 2 × domain share) for each the player steers. An institution the player belongs to below 10% counts a small control factor (P0: 0.05) in this upkeep: membership leaves a little mark (decided 2026-09-28, P0-36).
- **Stakes (decided 2026-09-27).** Each domain has established institutions (rivals) and room for one the player founds. The player **buys into** an established institution: the first purchase makes them a member with **1%**; each further 1% costs the domain's base price × (1 + stake% / 4) (steepened 2026-09-28), and the base differs by domain (P0: Medicine 2.4, Governance 3.6, Economy 3 gold, doubled 2026-09-28; tune), 2 Attention per purchase. **10%** counts toward influence but gives no oversight; **25%** gives a **voice** (priorities in its domain, economic policy through a Governance institution, a plague response (P0: once the plague's warnings have proved the inventor right, 10% of a Medicine or Governance institution suffices; decided 2026-09-28), and the institution pays a quarter of the player's projects in its domain, P0, tune); **50%** gives **oversight and control** (oversee, mentor, charter, endow, audit, invest). Dividends and shortfalls are shared by stake.
- **Joining requirements (decided 2026-09-28; a first version, to be built out later).** An established institution sets a condition on the player's **first** purchase. P0: the Physicians' Circle wants a finished Medicine project or the player's promise to its leader; the Tiber Island sanctuary asks nothing; the Caecilian faction wants patronage (consulting for wealthy households twice, or the senator's patronage project); the Junian faction wants property in Rome; the Merchants' Guild wants a business of the player's own; the banking house wants a first purchase of at least 5%. The two factions won't share a member: holding 10%+ of one shuts the player out of the other, and caps their stake in the other at 9% by purchase, seniority or reward (P0-31, decided 2026-09-28); holding 10%+ of one also makes the other obstruct the player's Governance projects (+50% cost). **Grievances (P0-31):** some choices cost standing with an institution the player hasn't joined; it remembers, and the grievance lowers the loyalty they start with if they join. Consulting for the rich costs standing with the Physicians' Circle; quarantine rules cost the Economy and the guild's goodwill; labor-saving inventions (wheelbarrow, horse collar, trip hammer) add Governance debt from unrest and cost the guild's goodwill; an endowed institution drifts faster (×1.5). The player's own institutions have no requirement.
- **Time and gold (decided 2026-09-28).** Influence in an established institution grows two ways. **Seniority:** each full year as a member with dues paid adds 1 percentage point of stake, up to **25%** (long membership can earn a voice, never control). **Gold:** newcomers pay a **premium** on each further 1%, ×3 on joining and fading to ×1 after 5 years of membership. Heavy gold buys influence fast; time makes it cheap. **Attending** meetings as a member (1 Attention, once a turn) raises the leader's regard; a member who attends at least twice a year earns 1 extra point of seniority that year. (tune) **Offices extend it (decided 2026-09-28):** 10% opens the way to office, and an office lets seniority keep growing past 25%: up to 30% for an officer, 40% for a deputy, 55% for a head, with 1 / 2 / 2 extra points a year, so leadership can grow into control (P0-36, tune).
- **Entry fee and dues (decided 2026-09-28).** Joining an established institution costs an **entry fee** on top of the first 1% (P0: sanctuary 5, Circle 10, guild 20, Junians 25, Caecilians 30, bank none because its 5% minimum deposit serves as one; tune). Members then pay **annual dues** that grow with their stake: a base per institution (P0: sanctuary 1, Circle 2, bank 2, guild 3, Junians 4, Caecilians 5 gold a year) + 0.1 gold a year per percent held (tune). Unpaid dues cost loyalty like an uncovered shortfall. The player's own institutions charge neither. A member below 10% pays half the dues (P0-36, tune). **Friends in several houses (decided 2026-09-28):** each established institution the player belongs to takes 4% off their projects (at most 16%) and adds 0.01 to plague resilience (at most 0.04) (P0-36, tune).
- **Inventions (P0, decided 2026-09-28; a first version).** The inventor can make inventions from future knowledge. Knowing is not making: each needs Means or Carriers from Rome (the workshop, a membership, a finished project). Each pays off in income, standing (its institution's leader loyalty) and influence (stake granted without purchase). P0: an invention tree of four branches of three tiers, one of them improving the workshop (decided 2026-09-28); stake and loyalty rewards vary by invention and reach every established institution; each after the first in its branch needs the one before it, and the whole tree is visible with locked ones marked. P0 list and amounts: data/content/inventions.json (tune). **P1 (Corey, implemented 2026-10-04): they are practical projects in the capability model.** No stake rewards: an institution you don't belong to takes an interest in you (regard), members' loyalty rises as before; a project may name the capability it moves when done (double-entry bookkeeping → recorded trials, demonstrated; the fever ward → comparative case records, demonstrated; water-powered bellows → graded tool steel, demonstrated) and can't start while that level is beyond Rome.
- **Camps and offices (decided 2026-09-28; P0 to build).** Each institution holds two **camps**, its two drift paths made visible while the inventor is present; attending a meeting is a vote for one camp, and the camp that leads at departure sets the drift path during the absence. The inventor can rise through **offices** with period titles (a trade association's quaestor or curator, decurio, and its president, the quinquennalis, elected every five years; a patron, patronus, from outside; a bank's institor and partner, socius; a great man's cliens, amicus and consilium; a chief physician, archiater; a temple's benefactor and warden, aedituus). A foreigner (peregrinus) can't hold the Senate's offices or a priesthood, so the factions stop at the consilium and the sanctuary at its warden. Office needs stake, seniority, the leader's regard and a vacancy or a vote; it gives weight in votes and powers over the institution and costs Attention every turn. **Last orders** at departure back a camp and, from the head's seat or in an institution the inventor founded, name a successor; their force scales with office, stake, the leader's loyalty to the inventor and the inventor's voting record. **What they change (revised 2026-09-28, Corey):** while the inventor is away, the camp that leads shapes the institution's upkeep of its domain (its first camp ×1.5, its second ×0.5 at full lean); while the inventor is a member or holds an office, it keeps the domain up each year (more from a higher office) and the institution pays a share of the inventor's projects in its domain (member 10%, officer 15%, deputy 25%, head 40%; membership added 2026-09-28 so ordinary membership pays a little); strong orders slow drift by up to 90%, and an institution that became the camp the inventor ordered, with real weight behind the order, returns Thriving. A founded institution may fail 7% a year while weak (was 15%). (tune; P0_PROPOSALS P0-32)
- **Founding** an institution of one's own costs **about 18%** of buying 0 → 50% of an established one in the same domain (decided 2026-09-28, Corey: found small, then grow it, toned down a little after the batch gate; was 40%, before that 65%) (P0: 3 Attention). The player controls it from the start, but it starts weak (strength 10, tune; was 15), holds little of its domain, and **may fail** (7% a year while strength is below 25, tune). Gold invested in an institution the player controls builds its strength (0.1 per gold, 2 Attention, tune). **Each has its own power (decided 2026-09-28)**, full at strength 40 and scaled below it, while it stands: the School of the Fountain trains physicians (plague resilience +0.05; Medicine target +2.5 during an absence); the Club of the Aventine carries the player's policy at a sway of at least 0.65, also after departure; Menodora's trading house pays its founder a dividend (3.5 gold a year × Economy ÷ its starting level) and its ships ease the late grain fleet (Economy target +1.5 during an absence) (toned down a little 2026-09-28, Corey) (P0-36, tune).
- **Influence and rivals.** An institution's **domain share** = its strength ÷ the strength of all institutions in the domain. **Control factor** = 0 below 10%, else min(1, stake ÷ 50%). The player's **influence** over a domain = Σ control factor × share; **sway** = min(1, 2 × influence) scales how much of a priority or policy takes effect. Rivals push back against an institution that **takes more of its domain**, not against the player's stake (decided 2026-09-28): an institution the player founded draws fire once it holds **20% of its domain**; an established one once it grows past the share it held at the start. Each rival's chance a year = 10% at the threshold + 2% per share point above it (max 75%) to cost that institution 3 strength (tune). Buying a stake alone provokes no one.
- **Leader integrity:** every institution leader has an integrity trait: honest, average, or venal.
- **Institution gold:** institutions hold gold given to them as endowments (see §9). An institution whose holdings reach the minimum endowment counts as endowed.
- **Debt payment during absence:** only in the **30 years after departure**, institutions pay down debt in the domains matching their type, at the 1.5× paydown premium, out of their holdings. Loyal institutions pay in full, drifted ones partially (50%, tune), rogue ones not at all. Payments reduce holdings.
- **Corruption:** checked each decade during the 30-year window only, separately from loyalty (loyalty decides whether an institution pays; corruption decides whether the gold survives).
  - Chance per decade = base hazard (0.05, tune) × exposure (×1 small holdings, ×2 large) × (1 − audit charter 0.5) × (1 − integrity: honest 0.3, average 0, venal −0.3).
  - Levels: **Minor** (lose 25% of holdings; debt payments continue at a reduced rate; small Governance debt); **Major** (lose 50%; payments stop; moderate Governance debt); **Total** (lose 100%; payments stop; large Governance debt; the institution becomes Captured or Rogue).
  - Level weights (Minor / Major / Total): unprotected 40 / 40 / 20; with an audit charter 70 / 25 / 5. A venal leader shifts weight toward Total, an honest leader toward Minor.
  - An **audit charter** (costs gold) can be founded as part of jump preparation. The pre-jump briefing shows corruption risk as Low / Medium / High and the potential severity, never the outcome; the arrival's Discovery beat reveals any corruption and its level.

## 8. Progression
- Stages: 1 Stranger; 2 Local Figure (livelihood + patron); 3 Founder (first institution; Influence Mode opens); 4 Power Broker (institutions on 2+ paths); 5 World Shaper (3+ paths, one abroad).
- At most one stage lost per negative event.
- **Long-run state during an absence:** Rome follows history's own ups and downs through each decade, and only the gap from history (the player's mark) moves toward its target (decided 2026-09-28: a player who left no mark tracks history rather than lagging its swings). Each domain moves toward its long-run target = historical baseline + k × (departure level − baseline at departure), with **k = 0.6** (was 0.3; P0-30, decided 2026-09-28), **from the day you leave**: **25% per decade** during the first 30 years (was 50% per decade toward the bare baseline) and 25% per decade after. This applies to deviations both above and below the baseline, so the region you leave keeps part of its lead or its deficit for centuries. Surviving institutions that maintain a domain add their maintenance on top (doubled, P0-30) and can hold it above the target. Institutional drift shows after 30 points (was 60), so a first arrival can already find an institution changed. (tune)
- Starting stage on arrival: 1 by default; 2 with a Legend or weak surviving institution; 3 maximum with a strong, loyal surviving institution.

## 9. Gold and fortune
- **P1 work (decided 2026-10-02, Corey): commissions, not a consulting grind.** Work grows encounter → help/diagnosis (stated as unpaid: "Pay: none initially · may lead to paid commission") → terms → staged work → paid completion → referral. Before substantial work starts the terms say who pays, how much and when, and who buys the materials (or that it is self-funded R&D, a favor or a profit share). Asking for more can win, meet a firm no, or lose the work, once per offer. A **ledger** records every change to the player's money with its counterparty and reason; the monthly settlement shows income, institutions' dues and costs, and upkeep separately. (P1 builds the first commission; the P0 odd/craft/consult work remains until it is retired.) **Generic work is a fallback (implemented 2026-10-04):** the menu offers only odd jobs, and only while no commission or Grand Challenge stage is under way; craft and consult remain typed commands for compatibility. Repeat work comes through people: Lollius's bilge pumps at Ostia follow the cellar pump once the inventor has been a guild guest. **More work across domains (implemented 2026-10-04, P1_PROPOSALS P1-17):** Pomponia's household fevers through Serenus (comparative records; medicine), Philippus's bath-house water (civil; a stingy client who usually walks if pushed; its fix later costs a fuller his tap), Diodoros's broken jars (logistics; a favor that pays in regard), Vettius's fitting drawings at Ostia (documentation; a profit share; needs guild membership and shared measures), and Statius's burnt mill bearing through Aulus. Jobs may be favors or profit shares, may move a capability per stage, may leave regard, standing or flags behind, and need not lead into any institution. **Later in the era (implemented 2026-10-04):** a second chance if the first job is lost (Aemilius's hoist, through Felix, also leading into the guild), and follow-up work from the same people (Lollius's crane, the Janiculum race for three mill owners, a second household for Serenus's patron's sister, Gaius's singing fountain as shared development, the Clivus drain for the street magistrates).
- Gold scales actions; institutional capacity limits how many.
- **Economic policy** (set through a political institution the player has a voice in, P0: the Caecilian or Junian faction or their own club; 2 Attention per change; every effect scales with sway over Governance, §7). **Rome keeps its historical practice unless the player drives it elsewhere (Corey, 2026-09-28):** freer markets come only from the player's policy or advocacy, and outlast the player only while an institution carries them. Four issues, each with an **Austrian stance**, Rome's **historical practice**, or an **interventionist stance**: coinage (sound / debase), prices (free / controlled), property (secure / discretionary), taxes (light / heavy). Austrian stances are better for the economy: each grows the Economy +1 a year against history and, during an absence, pulls its target up by more each decade the institution carrying it survives (P0-30: they build on themselves). They provoke **backlash** from those who profit from intervention: when adopted, −8 loyalty in the institution carrying it and +8 Governance debt; free prices while the plague rages add +5 Governance debt a year. Interventionist stances give a quick boom (+1.5 a year each) but build malinvestment that ends in a bust (§6); an interventionist policy left standing busts at most once per absence, after which its backers are discredited (P0-30). Taxes set the tax on work: light 5%, history 10%, heavy 20% (heavy also adds Governance +1 a year). Commercial institutions (P0: the Merchants' Guild of Ostia, the banking house of Octavius, or the player's own trading house) maintain the Economy. (tune) **Advocacy (decided 2026-09-28, Corey):** without a voice the player can still argue for a stance, in pamphlets, at dinners and in the Curia (P0: 12 gold, 2 Attention): the stance takes effect at a small sway (0.15, +0.03 for each membership, +0.05 for each rank of office in a Senate faction, at most 0.35) and only while the inventor is in Rome; an Austrian stance still provokes backlash. 10% of a Governance institution is not enough to set policy; it opens office instead (P0-36, tune).
- **Prices (decided 2026-09-28).** A price level rises each year with the debasement of the coin: Rome's historical practice debases slowly (P0: 1.5% a year), a debasement policy faster (4%), sound coin not at all, each adjusted by the player's sway over Governance. Everything bought with gold scales with it (projects, stakes, fees, dues, inventions, machine work); pay for work catches up only half as fast (tune).
- **Two currencies (decided 2026-09-28).** Gold isn't debased; Rome's silver coin is. Everyday money is **denarii** (1 aureus = 25 denarii in AD 155); **gold aurei** are a store of value whose price in denarii rises with the price level, so gold keeps its purchasing power (history's debasement unless the player's coinage policy changes it). Changing money is an action: a trip to the money changers (P0: 1 Attention, a 2.5% fee each way, tune). The inventor arrives with a purse of aurei scavenged from the machine and must change them to live; the machine takes gold back, not coin.
- **Gold across a jump (decided 2026-09-28).** The machine carries a small purse (P0: 10 aurei, tune). Beyond it, gold can be deposited with a banking house (P0: 2% a year simple interest in gold; a chance each decade that the house fails or its head embezzles, 8% × leader integrity, halved with 10% of the bank) or buried (no interest; 6% a decade that someone finds it). Anything else in hand is lost. **Limits (decided 2026-09-28):** the bank refuses more than 100 aurei from one depositor and a jar holds 50 (P0: tune), and bigger sums are riskier: the bank's loss chance × (1 + deposit ÷ 100), the jar's × (1 + hoard ÷ 50). The briefing shows risk bands, never outcomes; the arrival's Discovery beat reveals what became of the gold.
- **The coin and Rome's decline (decided 2026-09-28).** Debasement is one of the downfalls of Rome. Rome's own debasement follows the historical silver content of its coin (about 78% in AD 155, about 50% by 200, a few percent by 268; authored estimates), and **near half** of the Economy's historical decline to the coin's low (AD 155–268, the plague's step left out) is the coin's, with a smaller share of Governance (P0: a quarter; the army's pay, mutinies and usurpers; tune). The coin's share falls as the silver does. While a Governance institution carrying the player's coinage policy stands, sound coin spares Rome that share of the debasement after departure (scaled by sway) and a debasement policy adds to it (P0: half again at full sway, tune); once no one defends the coin, Rome debases as history did. The arrival shows the coin in the Wrongness beat.
- **No passive income without leverage.** The inventor earns gold by working (better-paid work costs more Attention) and from property they funded and own, which grows with the Economy. Each institution earns its own rate × the Economy level × strength, so margins are thin until it is established. It pays its own running cost (different per institution; none once endowed) **and its part of the upkeep of the player's priority for its domain**. The inventor receives their stake's share of its surplus and covers their stake's share of any shortfall. The inventor never pays domain upkeep directly. **Memberships** bring customers and patrons: each established institution the player belongs to raises their work pay 10% (P0, tune). (tune)
- Institutions hold a **share of the regional economy**. Value = share × regional economy.
- **During the 30-year window after departure**, holdings are a balance growing at the regional economic growth rate (0–1.5% per year by the Economy level; equivalent to a fixed share of a growing economy), never a fixed interest rate. **After the window, holdings freeze in P0.** The full share model arrives at P3.
- Access on arrival by loyalty: high = regular draws; medium = negotiated; low = token; rogue = none.
- **Loss = Hazard × Exposure × (1 − Resilience).** Hazards may be random; exposure and resilience come from player choices.
- Larger shares raise confiscation and revolution risk; aggressive extraction adds stability debt; longer jumps increase exposure.

## 10. People
- **P1 (decided 2026-10-02, Corey): recurring people have lives of their own.** Each has a goal, a vulnerability, people they care about, someone they distrust, a household, status, opinions, an outside interest and a voice, and their lives go on without the player: illness, fire, loss, a new job, a bad copy of the player's work. A life event happens once, when its requirements in the game hold, by seeded chance each month (P1_PROPOSALS P1-08). **No inevitability (implemented 2026-10-04):** an event may have a window (months after it first becomes possible), a declining monthly hazard and a group of mutually exclusive branches (after the fire Diodoros either takes over the deliveries or goes home to Antioch, or neither), and a person can leave Rome for good. A person who is ill or away can't invite, sponsor or introduce; a copy made by someone else spreads a capability without the player, possibly distorted. Every change is logged with its causes and the people who acted. P1 people: data/content/people.json (Felix, Cassianus, Diodoros, Serenus, Gaius, Livia, Marcus, Lucan, Aulus, Sextus). **Implemented 2026-10-04:** each has offscreen developments (offices, debts, betrothals, a mother's death, a public quarrel, a refused partnership, a shop of one's own or a foreman's post, failing eyes and death, an injured back) and private scenes that come through the router from world state; some put a choice to the player (witness a marriage contract; lend Felix money), and people can say no. The legacy P0 smith is renamed Successus so that Marcus Fabius Tertius is the only Tertius.
- Nudge success = Trust × receptivity.
- Trust is explained by remembered interactions drawn from the event log.
- **Visibility** rises with how anachronistic, public, and threatening an act is, and with displays of wealth. Threat chance ∝ Visibility × threat to power.
- **Legacy retained on death:** 30% base; +20% per trained student (max 2); +20% if written; +10% if held by an institution.
- **Divergence:** chance a later historical figure appears as documented = 1 − (regional divergence × sensitivity); otherwise altered or lost, possibly replaced by an echo figure.
- **Promises:** at most 2 active; breaking one affects relationships, descendants, institution loyalty, Legend, and the Chronicle.

- **Experiment feedback:** physical observations over a core that reveals **how many** parts are correct, never which. A specialist gets at most one extra inference per experiment in their domain.
- **Repair tiers:**

- The player is never forced to jump.

- **Arrival:** recognition → wrongness → personal echo → discovery. **Walking around (decided 2026-09-28):** at every arrival, including the first, the player can visit a few places that show present conditions and what changed since they left, in words and numbers; never causes. ## 12. Index
- Sub-score per domain = world value ÷ historical value at the same date × 100.
- Overall Index = geometric mean of sub-scores (rewards balance).
- Sphere Index (player's regions) and World Index (globe).

## 13. Knowledge
- Knowledge lives in regions and spreads by diffusion; the world advances on its own.
- Nodes: prerequisites (alternative paths allowed), Means, Carriers, effects, shadow effects, historical window, fuel or repair link.
- Reusable capability layers sit behind nodes.
- **P1 (decided 2026-10-02, Corey): the hidden capability network.** Separate from what the inventor knows, Rome holds a level in each capability: none → theory → demonstrated → prototype → reproducible → manufacturable → economical → adopted → institutionalized. Every node starts from what Rome already did well (its baseline), names the bottleneck, and the inventor's leap (formalize, improve, combine, scale, standardize, apply), never "invent" what Rome had. A capability can't pass a level its prerequisites haven't reached, up to reproducible (P1_PROPOSALS P1-07); **a dependency edge may name the level it needs** (implemented 2026-10-04: e.g. steam needs graded tool steel manufacturable, interchangeable parts and recorded trials reproducible), which applies once the target is a working thing (prototype or beyond). **Maturity and spread are separate (Corey, implemented 2026-10-04):** the level is the true maturity of the method done right; imitation records how far the work spread (local / copied / widespread), whether the copies are distorted and whether they carry the wrong name, and never raises the level. Work moves it: each commission stage advances its capability (the cellar pump takes true valve seats to reproducible), logged with its cause. Hidden in play; the Knowledge and Civilization views show only what is relevant. P1 graph: data/content/capabilities.json (16 nodes).
- **P1 (decided 2026-10-02, Corey): Grand Challenges.** Small problems prove principles; big projects change capability. A challenge opens when the work raises its question. **No challenge hangs on one client (hardening pass, 2026-10-04):** a challenge lists several routes, each a real encounter with its problem (all of a route's requirements must hold; any route will do); they converge on the one challenge, which opens once, by the first route that holds in authored order, logged with that route's scene. P1: "Can another workshop make a part that fits without seeing the original?" comes up through the cellar pump's worn seat, Aemilius's hoist pawl cut by another smith, or Philippus's stopcock plug turned by another plumber. Its few chunky stages are self-funded projects the player starts (gold paid, Attention held each month) that move capabilities; once the bottleneck is understood there are no micro-scenes. A stage can be held up by people (no one to vouch for you, or they are away), by what Rome can do (prerequisites), or by a **resource**: through its source (a relationship or membership) it costs the stated price, without it a multiple (P1_PROPOSALS P1-10). P1 content: data/content/challenges.json (Measurement and standards, four stages). **Powered workshops (implemented 2026-10-04):** "Can one power source reliably drive several useful workshop machines, with components and maintenance Roman craftsmen can reproduce?" Rome already had water power, gears, bearings and lathes; the leap is to combine, standardize and scale. It opens once gauges are reproducible and the inventor knows the millwright Aulus; six stages (a site on the Janiculum race, replaceable bearing blocks, one real machine, the load problem, a second different task, three months under load) move line shafts, replaceable bearing blocks, controlled transmission and powered boring and grinding (three new capability nodes). A stage may name a stand-in (Marcus, else Gaius, two months slower) and move several capabilities. When it is done its human consequence follows as a triggered choice, not an output bonus: Sextus Nerius asks what happens to his twelve men (P1_PROPOSALS P1-15). Life can delay a stage (Gaius closes the shop for his son's naming day), and Marcus may leave for a rival shop or stay, depending on how he has been treated.
- Emergent variants come from rule tables: base node × modifier (carrier, culture, distortion) → bounded variant with at least one trade-off.

## 14. Hard constraints (enforced in code and content checks)
- Player institutions can never use enslaved labor.
- No atrocity actions (massacre, ethnic cleansing) exist as player verbs.
- Religious founders are never depicted, nudged, or erased; the existence of major world religions is fixed.
- No project, advancement, or verb gives the player a weapon of mass destruction as a tool; such outcomes occur only as consequences.
- Exploitation always produces a net Index loss.
