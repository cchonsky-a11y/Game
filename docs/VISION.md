# VISION.md — The Butterfly Effect

> Long-term vision. This file describes what the game is and what must be protected.
> It is **not** an implementation list. What to build now is defined only in PROTOTYPE_SCOPE.md.

## Pitch
A strategy game about teaching the past enough of the future to get yourself home, then living with what the past did with your ideas.

## Premise
A 21st-century inventor launched an unfinished time machine after dismissing a warning. It malfunctioned and stranded them in Rome, AD 155. To get home they must advance civilization far enough to repair the machine and produce its missing fuel, one era at a time, across 8 eras. Every change ripples forward.

## Core emotional promise
The player leaves an era, returns centuries later, and finds a world their actions changed. The game shows the change; it doesn't have to prove the link.

## Target player
Civilization players who want more depth on Apple platforms (iPhone, iPad, Mac); secondary appeal to history and story fans. Real depth, clear and approachable, layered over time. No aggressive free-to-play mechanics.

## Tone
Like the Civilization series: serious and historically grounded, accessible, with room for lighter moments.

## Design pillars (ranked)
1. **Every Choice Echoes** — the player's actions visibly change the world.
2. **Knowing Is Not Making** — future knowledge never bypasses materials, tools, skills, institutions, or adoption capacity.
3. **One Person in a Large World** — the inventor stays personally limited and at risk; the world has its own will.

Pillar 1 beats Pillar 2; Pillar 2 beats Pillar 3.

**Guiding rule:** the player has the fun, not the simulation.
**Presentation rule:** simulation underneath, human dilemma on top.

## Design constitution
1. Player actions must visibly change the world.
2. The inventor never directly controls civilizations.
3. Personal Attention never scales with power.
4. Knowledge never bypasses Means and Carriers.
5. Neglect must arise from understandable trade-offs.
6. Simulation complexity must surface as decisions, not bookkeeping.
7. Every jump must materially transform the world.
8. Failure should create history, not merely subtract progress.
9. Major institutions are represented through people.
10. No feature exists merely because other historical strategy games have it.

## Protected ideas
- **Legibility in the moment:** "Why?" explains current conditions during an era. After a jump, the world simply shows what changed; no causal tracing is required.
- **Absorption:** every idea (technology, law, finance, reform) needs Knowledge, Means, and Carriers.
- **Institutions as indirect control:** civilization-scale action ("Influence Mode") happens only through organizations the inventor built or influenced, each with a leader and an evolving identity.
- **The jump as the centerpiece:** a persistent temptation, and a four-beat arrival (recognition, wrongness, personal echo, discovery), with an optional Learn more.
- **Echoes and promises:** emotional continuity across centuries.

## Never
- Paying to bypass a trade-off (time skips, resources, puzzle hints, protection from risk or death).
- Premium currency, loot boxes, energy systems, timers, or ads.
- Atrocity actions (enforced in code and content checks, not just guidelines); player institutions using enslaved labor; depicting or erasing religious founders.
- The language model deciding outcomes. The simulation decides; the model only describes.

## Priority order when trading off
Visible impact → Absorption → Institutions → Jump payoff → everything else.
