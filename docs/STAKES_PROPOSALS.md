## Slice 1a: a life that can be spent, and lost

Slice 1 is split in two. 1a is the inventor's own body: age, illness, death. 1b (Notice: what Rome thinks you are, and the first threat events) follows.

| Id | Area | Proposal | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-01 | Life budget | The inventor's age is shown wherever the date is shown, with the healthy years left: "Age 36 · about 49 healthy years left". Starts at 28 (SYSTEMS §3); healthy years **85** (Corey, 2026-11-10; proposed as 70). One year older for each year lived in an era; none during a jump (canon). The departure briefing states it as a fact: "You are 40. You have spent 12 of your 57 healthy years in Rome." No new mechanic in Rome beyond S-02 to S-06; the point is that the player sees what staying costs. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-02 | Catching the plague | When an outbreak is resolved while the inventor is in Rome, the inventor may fall ill. Chance = **3.5** × the share of Rome that dies in that outbreak, at most 0.6 (history's 10% dead gives 35%; a contained 3% gives about 10%). (Proposed as 3; raised on Corey's "a little higher".) Precautions the inventor has actually taken each take a fifth off: the district fountain repaired; soap and boiled linen; quarantine rules or the quarantine response; a fever ward. All four: chance × 0.2. Drawn from the seeded generator; logged with the outbreak as its cause. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-03 | The illness | Ill: 0 Attention for 2 months, then −1 for 4 months (SYSTEMS §3 already has −1 when unwell). Work under way pauses, and people react through their existing lives (a client may walk; a friend may sit with you). 3 healthy years are lost for good. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-04 | Dying of it | Chance of death once ill: **0.30** (proposed as 0.25; raised on Corey's "a little higher"). Care lowers it: a physician who knows you (Serenus) −0.08; membership of the Physicians' Circle −0.05; distilled wine for wounds done −0.04; the Tiber Island sanctuary's benefactor −0.03. Never below 0.05. A careless player who stays through the outbreak: about 1 game in 10 ends here. A careful one: under 1 in 100. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-05 | Leaving the city | From the plague's second warning, a new choice: withdraw to the country until it passes (as the rich did). Cost: no work and no Attention in Rome for the months away (proposed 6), 15 aurei at AD 155 prices, and those who stayed remember it (regard −1 with people who know you well; a promise to Demetria counts as broken if it needed you there). Effect: the chance of falling ill × 0.25. Leaving by machine before the outbreak avoids it entirely: the first real reason to jump early. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-06 | Ordinary fevers | Each late summer in Rome (Sextilis–September), a 6% chance of the Roman fever: 0 Attention for 1 month, −1 the next. No death from it under 50; from 50, a 3% chance, lowered by the same care as S-04. Chance +1 point for each year of age over 40. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-07 | A run that ends | Death without a successor ends the run (canon: SYSTEMS §3, GDD §14). Succession and saves are not built, so in this prototype death is final. The game does not stop on a blank screen: it runs the same absence a jump would (drawn from the range the machine had), then shows what Rome kept of the inventor (the people echoes, what the work became, the journal's lines) under a closing line with the age and year of death. No arrival, no return places. The seed and the replay file allow the same game to be started again. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |
| S-08 | Saying what can end the run | The status screen and the plague's warnings say it plainly once it is true: "You can catch this." The departure briefing and the outbreak's decision card show the chance as a band (low / moderate / high), never the number or the outcome, as for other risks. | **Approved 2026-11-10, built** |

**What this changes for existing tests.** As built, health draws from its own seeded generator derived from the run's seed, so the snapshot hash did not change and other draws were not reshuffled; determinism (same seed and inputs, same log) is kept and tested. The batch strategies and scripted profiles stay in Rome (they never withdraw); a death ends their run and is counted.

**Open questions for Corey.**
1. Is about 7 in 100 deaths for an unprepared player the right order of size, or should a first death be rarer (or commoner)?
2. S-05: is fleeing the city something the inventor should be able to do, given the promise to Demetria?
3. S-07: is "the run ends and the game shows what Rome kept" right for now, or should a death restart the era?

## Slice 1b: Notice (what Rome thinks you are)

Replaces the unbuilt single "Visibility" meter (SYSTEMS §10). No Roman can conclude the inventor is from the future; they explain the inventor with the categories they have. Everything here is drawn from what the game already logs. Wonders (slice 5) will add to it later.

| Id | Area | Proposal | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| S-10 | Notice | How much Rome talks about the inventor, in four steps: **unknown → known in the district → talked about → notorious**. It rises with public acts: each finished commission +1, each Grand Challenge stage +1 and a finished challenge +3, each practical project (the old inventions) +2, a public project +2, an office +3, standing at a senator's salutatio +2, the plague response +3, a foreknowledge proved right +3. Steps at 6, 16 and 30. For the first six months it cannot pass "unknown" (the Early reputation rule). It never falls on its own. | Proposed |
| S-11 | Readings | The same acts feed six readings, and the largest is what Rome takes the inventor for (ties: the older one stands). **Craftsman:** commissions, challenge stages, workshop orders, the guild. **Healer:** medicine work, the Circle, the plague response. **Prophet:** acting against the plague before its first warning; the promise kept; foreknowledge proved right. **Magus:** work on the machine that others could notice (each repair step done +1; `listen` +2), a labor-saving device, a house with no patron and no guild. **The senator's man:** client of Varro, a Senate faction's office. **Fraud:** a job walked away from or lost, a refused admission vote, a broken promise. Shown as one line: "Rome takes you for a craftsman. Some say a healer." (the second-largest, if at least half the largest). | Proposed |
| S-12 | Protection | Three things stand between the inventor and trouble, each worth one point: a patron (Varro's client), a house that would speak for you (member of the guild or the Circle), and a clean name (no Fraud points). Shown with the reading: "A senator's house and the guild would speak for you." | Proposed |
| S-13 | The accusation | Once "talked about", with the reading Healer, Prophet or Magus, someone may bring a charge (poisoning for a healer, divination for a prophet, magic for a magus). It needs an accuser: a client who walked or was lost, a house that holds a grievance, or a death under the inventor's care (the plague response when the toll was Severe or worse). Chance each month while those hold: 2%, 4% when notorious. **Warning first:** one month before, "people are saying it in the street", and the player may act (below). | Proposed |
| S-14 | The hearing | A decision, with the cost of each answer stated: **let your patron speak** (needs a patron; the house then asks a favor), **pay the accuser off** (30 aurei at AD 155 prices; +2 Fraud), **answer the charge yourself**, or **leave Rome before it is heard** (the machine if it is ready, or the country as in S-05: a year away, and the charge stands when you return). Answering yourself is settled by chance: acquitted 50% + 15 points per protection (S-12). Otherwise: a fine and your work sealed for 6 months (two thirds of the rest), or **exile from the city for 3 years** (a quarter), or, with no protection at all and a capital charge (magic, poisoning), **death** (the remainder, about 1 hearing in 12 of those lost). | Proposed |
| S-15 | The trade's anger | A labor-saving device (the wheelbarrow, the horse collar, the trip hammer, powered workshops) without guild membership: 5% a month for a year that men who lost work come at night. Warning first (a threat chalked on the door). Then: the work under way loses 3 months and a stage's materials are paid again. Guild membership turns it into a hearing before the guild, with no damage. | Proposed |
| S-16 | Being kept | Once notorious, a great house sends for the inventor (Varro's if a client, else the palace's steward). **Accept:** 2 Attention every month go to the house (a standing duty, like an office), it pays 4 aurei a month, and it counts as a patron. **Refuse:** the house holds a grievance (an accuser for S-13). It is a burden, not an ending: the machine still takes you away. Being kept is stated in the departure briefing as something you leave. | Proposed |
| S-17 | Said plainly | The status screen shows the Notice step, the reading and the protection. A warning says what is coming and what can be done. Chances are never shown as numbers; the hearing shows "your position: weak / fair / strong". | Proposed |

**What this adds to losing.** Death by hearing is rare and needs three failures at once (a capital reading, no protection, a lost hearing). Exile and sealed work are the common costs. Exile for 3 years in a 20-year era is a real setback but not an ending.

**What it does not do yet.** No wonders (slice 5), no theft or fire at the machine, no citizenship, no attribution verb ("let the guild take the credit"). Each needs content of its own.

**Settled 2026-11-11 (on Corey's behalf, under his delegation; he may overrule): S-10 to S-17 are approved as written; exile is 3 years and the machine stays reachable; death by hearing stays, rare; the palace only once notorious (Corey).**

**The questions as they were asked.**
1. S-14: is exile the right heavy penalty, and is 3 years the right size? During exile the inventor is outside Rome: no work, as in the country, but the machine is in Rome. Proposed: the inventor may still slip back to the machine and jump.
2. S-13 and S-14: should death by hearing exist at all at this stage, or should the worst outcome be exile until wonders are in?
3. S-16: is "the palace" too early for AD 155–175, given the rule that the first months are socially minor? Proposed: only once notorious, which no measured profile reaches before year 5.
