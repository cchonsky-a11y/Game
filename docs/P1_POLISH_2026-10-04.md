## 1. Wealth across jumps (built: clarity only, no rule change)

Some wealth reaches the future on purpose; some does not.

| Kind of wealth | What happens at a jump | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Aurei up to the machine's purse (10) | Carried and arrive with you | `P1WealthAcrossJumpsTests` |
| Aurei beyond the purse | Left behind and lost, unless deposited or buried | same |
| Deposited with the banking house (up to 100) | Returned with simple interest, or lost if the house fails or its head embezzles. The briefing shows a risk band, never the outcome. | same; `MachineTests` |
| Buried in a jar (up to 50) | Found intact (no interest), or gone if someone dug it up | same |
| Denarii in hand | Stay behind (logged and in the ledger). Change them into aurei, or spend them, first. | `P1EconomyStressTests`, `P1WealthAcrossJumpsTests` |
| Money institutions hold | Stays with them in Rome and works there (debt paydown and growth for 30 years). It is never part of the purse and never comes back as coin. | `P1WealthAcrossJumpsTests` |

The earlier bug, stated precisely: **unconverted denarii in hand survived the jump automatically.** The intended channels were never affected.

The departure briefing now groups all of this in one block: carry; too much to carry, with the deposit or bury terms; the bank; the jar; coin in hand; institutions' money. Endowing is offered only to someone who controls an institution.

## 2. Event and effect validation (built)

Content loading now also rejects, with a message:
- unknown decision-event requirements;
- duplicate option ids;
- unknown effect types;
- bad domains on `level` and `debt` effects;
- unknown institutions on `loyalty`, `lean`, `stake` and `standing` effects;
- unknown people and missing status text on `regard` and `status` effects;
- `if` conditions naming an institution that isn't the player's own, or a flag nothing sets;
- unknown mark institutions;
- workshop orders' effects and size requirements;
- invention requirements, effect types, domains and groups;
- machine step requirements.

Tests keep the effect-type lists in step with the code that applies them, and check that every fast-forward notable-event type is one the simulation records.

## 3. The 49-month progression wait (investigated; inert switch built)

**What waited.** The worst case was the Physicians' Circle's guest-supper invitation (seed 52, Cooperative). Other long waits were Lollius's crane, the Clivus drain, Vettius's drawings, Felix's son, and Cassianus's daughter's betrothal.

**Why.**
- The two-in-a-row rule barred the waiting scene in only **2.4%** of its waiting months, across all 149 waits of 25+ months.
- Player focus played no part for cooperative players.
- The pool is crowded: 15–25 candidates a month, progression at weight 3, texture at 1, and no memory of how long something has waited.
- In the worst case, other categories were open every month, and the guild's own invitations were picked four times meanwhile.

**Does the player feel it?** Yes, for invitations. A person whose conditions the player can see are met, and who then doesn't come back for three or four years, reads as the game forgetting. It matters less for late-era work and offscreen lives.

**Options weighed:**

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. Age bonus | Fits the cause. **Built, off:** `scenes.progressionAgePerMonth`, PROPOSED P1-23, default 0, so routing is unchanged. Counterfactual at 0.10: longest wait 49 → 31, mean 7.3 → 5.9; Roman-life share, commissions and odd jobs unchanged. At 0.25: 26 and 4.9. |
| B. Exempting long waits from the two-in-a-row rule | Doesn't address the cause |
| C. Re-categorizing scenes | Doesn't address the cause |
| D. No change | Acceptable for work and lives, not ideal for invitations |

**Recommendation.** Corey turns on A at about 0.10. That changes every seeded game and the snapshot, so it needs his approval. 3:1 stays.

## 4. Fallback work (presentation fixed; economics unchanged)

- **Odd jobs are a safety net for cooperative play:** 14–23 of 96 months, 14–21% of income.
- **They become the main economy only for players who refuse or lose work:** the Negotiator on the fountain, at 35% of income; the Engineering-focused fountain player, at 47%, because it refuses the fountain's own civic and neighborhood jobs. This is a deliberate consequence.
- **The grind feeling came from presentation.** One identical sentence printed every month. Odd jobs now rotate through lines tied to the opening and to the people the inventor knows, in a fixed order with no random draw.
- **No new commissions were added.** The referral and repeat chains already cover both openings.

## 5. Political access (foundation built; the following stays OPEN)

**The chain:**
- useful work, then a trusted intermediary: Cassianus (who supplies Senator Varro's house) or Serenus (called to its sickroom), after month 18;
- an introduction to the freedman steward Hermogenes;
- standing at the morning salutatio as one client among forty;
- a favor asked: speak for a fuller-client in a water dispute. Obliging, measuring first and declining each cost something:
  - the Caecilians' regard against the Junians';
  - Gaius, if the inventor measured honest taps with him;
  - the steward's regard.

**What it is not:** membership, stake, office, policy voice or historical importance.

**Next steps (OPEN, for Corey):**
- whether and how a client becomes part of the following (the Caecilian house's offices: cliens, amicus, consilium);
- whether the factions' exclusivity applies to clients;
- whether the Junian house gets its own intermediary;
- how political obligations recur after the first favor.

**Machine archive step (OPEN).** It asks for faction membership, which P1 never grants, so it always costs its gold alternative. Options:
1. A patron's introduction is enough: the steward can let a client into the house's records.
2. Keep membership, which is unreachable in P1.
3. Another route: the Tabularium or a public library, through Serenus or the guild.

## 6. Human echoes and elapsed time (design only, not built)

**Today.** A person's echo lines are chosen by arrival number: `fromJump` is 1 or 2. That fits jumps of 25–60 years. It breaks for long jumps: a line written for "first arrival" assumes the person may still be alive, whether 30 or 150 years have passed.

**The refactor needed:**
1. **Years since the inventor last saw the person**, not the arrival count. The arrival already knows `JumpYears`; keep a per-person "last seen" year.
2. **A life model per person:** a birth year (or age at meeting) plus an expected lifespan. From that, whether the person is alive, elderly or dead at the arrival year. Deaths that already happened in play (Lucan) are fixed.
3. **Echo lines tagged by generation, not arrival:**

   | Tag | Years since last seen | Who carries the echo |
   |---|---|---|
   | `self` | about 0–40 | the person themselves |
   | `elder` | about 30–60 | the person, elderly or recently dead |
   | `heirs` | about 50–100 | children, apprentices, the shop or house |
   | `memory` | about 80+ | descendants, institutions, documents, legend, misattribution |

   A line is eligible when the elapsed years fall in its band, which replaces `fromJump`. The existing lines map cleanly: `fromJump 1` → self or elder; `fromJump 2` → heirs or memory.
4. **Institutional memory as a fallback carrier.** If a member house survives, it can carry the person's echo when no relative can (the guild's founders' feast already does this).
5. **No new randomness.** Bands are deterministic from years; the choice among eligible lines stays as it is (not yet featured, then regard, then authored order).

The data change touches people.json (birth year and a band per echo line) and `PeopleEchoes`. Wait until the jump range itself is redesigned (section 9).

## 7. Grand Challenge route causality (audited; no change)

**How it works.** `OpeningRoute` picks the first route in authored order (pump, hoist, allotment, baths) whose requirements hold on the month the router picks the challenge's scene.

**Can credit go to the wrong event?** Only if a later-listed route became eligible first and an earlier-listed one also held by the time the scene was picked. For example:
- the allotment route holds from month 12;
- the challenge scene waits;
- the pump is finished later;
- the pump route now wins the credit.

**Does the player see a contradiction?** No. Every route's text describes an encounter that really happened in that game. The pump's worn seat is true whenever the pump is done, so no sentence is false. Observed in 300 runs: pump 288, hoist 8, allotment 3, never 1. The edge case needs both a fountain allotment and a pump, so it is rare.

**If Corey wants strict causality**, the narrow fix is to record the route that first became eligible. The router already remembers when each candidate began waiting (`CandidateSince`), so a route id could be stored alongside it. That changes which scene some games show, so it is not done here.

## 8. Attention: NEEDS HUMAN VALIDATION (not retuned)

- Scripted measurements: about a third of months end with no Attention free; challenge stages wait for Attention 1–5 times a run; 40–55% of months leave 2 or more unused; no future month is ever overbooked.
- The scripts underuse optional social actions (meetings, gifts, Roman-life spending, the salutatio), so idle Attention overstates what a person would leave.
- Total, costs, reservation and the overbooking checks are unchanged.

## 9. Jump range (unchanged; what must change before longer jumps)

The current range is 25–40 years from repairs, plus upgrades and time in the era, capped at 60. The 300-run spread was 25–55 years.

Before 80–100-year jumps (like the ~83-year jump in an early live session) are safe:
1. **Human echoes** need the elapsed-time model (section 6).
2. **Carried capabilities** climb one rung per 20 years, so 80–100 years would carry anything reproducible to adopted or institutionalized. That needs a ceiling or a decay of carriers, such as institutions dissolving along the way.
3. **History content** thins after about AD 250: the crisis of the third century, Gallienus and the coin's collapse are curves without scenes. Arrival text for those years would be thin.
4. **Institutions:** the 30-year windows for compounding, debt paydown and corruption close early, and the rest of a long absence is pure drift. That is fine mechanically, but the arrival beats need text for houses that changed hands twice.
5. **Generations:** no model of heirs or successors for people yet (section 6). Institutions have successor leaders.
6. **Range model:** the A, B and C options in the strategy-matrix report stay OPEN.

## 10. Final P1 polish assessment (judged beyond tests)

**Opening.** Clear and short. No tutorial dump: the machine, the cart, "Ancient Rome. Not ruins. Alive.", then the hour-one choice.

**Work:**
- Payer, materials and terms are explicit, and negotiation matters (jobs are lost).
- Fallback work is a net: not dominant for players who take work, and no longer word-for-word repetitive.
- Players who refuse work are poorer, by design.

**Relationships.** Recurring people have lives, refuse, leave and copy. Their regard changes routing of echoes and stage stand-ins. They ask things of the player (Cassianus's seal, Felix's loan, Hermogenes's favor).

**Rome.** Roman life is about half of routed scenes and present every year. Dialogue is concrete. The historical baseline is respected.

**Technology.** Romans are competent; nodes start from Roman practice. The Grand Challenges are real bottlenecks (shared measures; power to several machines) with several ways in.

**Institutions:**
- The guild and the Circle are entered by invitation; the sanctuary by gifts; the bank keeps legitimate stakes.
- A senator's house is now reachable as a client through people. The following stays closed (OPEN).

**Attention.** Hard limits, no overbooking. Whether it binds enough needs people.

**Machine.** The repair path is coherent. R-17 stays unexplained. DO NOT JUMP is a warning, not a lock.

**Jump:**
- Wealth handling is clear.
- Technical echoes come from capabilities carried by surviving houses.
- Human echoes go to the people the inventor was closest to.
- Institutional outcomes are named.
- Unintended echoes come from bad copies that happened in play: 297 of 300 first arrivals have all four echo kinds.
- No repeated text: 0 repeated sentences between arrivals and 0 duplicate scene texts across 300 runs.
