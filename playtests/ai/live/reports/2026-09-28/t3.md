# Playtest report: t3

**Persona:** casual player, new to strategy games. Skims long text, uses the numbered menu, gets impatient with bookkeeping.
**Seed:** 613 (final hash ce5f4e5bc8ff46c3)
**Length:** about 130 commands; Rome era AD 155–170 (92 turns), jump to AD 220, second jump to AD 260.

## Key choices, with years
- **Hour one (AD 155):** fixed the Subura fountain instead of buying into the workshop. It cost 40 of my 60 aurei. I bought into the smith's workshop anyway in AD 156 (975 denarii).
- **Machine:** assessed it AD 155–156. Finished all 9 repair steps by AD 163; joining the guild and the Junians made some steps cheap or free. Bought back the last 40 aurei in AD 167. No upgrades.
- **Institutions:**
  - Physicians' Circle: joined AD 155 (I qualified through the fountain). Reached 10% in AD 159 and a 25% voice in AD 166 (from the fever ward reward). Became senior physician in AD 167, then second to the archiater in AD 169. 34% when I left.
  - Merchants' Guild: joined AD 158 (20% at departure).
  - Junian faction: joined AD 158, mainly for free chronometer repairs (12% at departure).
  - Never founded my own institution.
  - Votes: always the Hospice of the Fountain in the Circle, the Free Traders in the guild, the Junian Bench once.
- **Inventions:** soap (AD 156), distilled wine for wounds (AD 157), fever ward with case records (AD 166).
- **Projects:** quarantine rules (AD 160; cost Economy −3), market with honest weights and free prices (AD 163), physician (AD 164), midwives (AD 166–167), mint audit (AD 168).
- **Policy:** never used policy or advocacy. Set Medicine to Protect once I had a voice (AD 166).
- **Rome's choices:**
  - Tiber flood (161): paid for relief.
  - Parthian war levy (162): paid.
  - Galen (162): sponsored him against my own Circle.
  - Tertius selling designs (163): let him sell.
  - Sabinus's grain cartel (164): refused.
  - Tertius's river forge (168): let the guild lend.
  - Palace auction (169): paid for relief instead of buying a gold cup.
- **Promise:** Demetria asked me to stay through the pestilence (AD 165). I said yes and kept it.
- **Workshop:** 3 apprentices (2 of them hired by accident), took orders now and then, never expanded it myself.
- **Plague:** warnings in AD 165–166; outbreak Sept AD 166. I chose the hospice response. Contained: about 20,000 dead, 2% of Rome (history: 10%).
- **Jump:** left in March AD 170, carrying 9 aurei, with 50 buried. Landed in AD 220 (a 50-year jump). Jumped again to AD 260.

## The four questions
1. **What were you choosing between during the Rome era?**
   - Mostly between earning money and spending Attention on things that last: consulting for cash versus inventing, buying stakes or running projects.
   - Health versus trade: quarantine rules helped Medicine but cost the Economy.
   - Loyalty to my own Circle versus backing Galen, whom I knew history would vindicate.
   - Quick profit versus goodwill: the grain cartel, the gold cup versus bread for the poor, selling my designs.
   - Leaving early, once the machine was fixed in AD 163, versus keeping my promise to stay through the plague.
2. **What changed in the world you came back to?**
   - Rome was much bigger (1.33 million people instead of about 980,000), healthier, and full of children.
   - The fountain still ran clean.
   - Healers used soap, spirits and fever wards.
   - The smith's shop had become a whole street of forges.
   - The Circle still met by the fountain, run by the Hospice camp I backed.
   - The coin was debased and prices had doubled, then quadrupled by AD 260.
   - The guild had become the grain cartel I refused, and the Junians had become landlords.
3. **Did any of that feel connected to you? Which part?**
   - Yes, strongly: "AQUA PURA" on the fountain, "For the one who stayed" over the Circle's door, and my misspelled name on the officers' roll and on the war-levy tablet.
   - "Hospes, the stranger" beside the flood line, for the flood relief I paid.
   - My inventions still in use, and the plague (and the later ones) hitting far softer than history.
   - The cartel also felt connected, in the sense that I lost that fight.
4. **Would you jump again to see what happens next?** Yes. I did jump a second time, and I'd go again. I want to know whether the Hospice survives and whether anything stops the coin sinking.

## Bugs, dead ends, confusing text
- Empty text in the invention list: `the guild:  (if you're a member)`, with nothing after "guild:". It appears on the wheelbarrow, collar and trip hammer.
- Menu labels aren't typeable commands: the menu says `join guild`, but typing `join guild` gives "Unknown command". The same goes for plain `consult` (it needs `work consult`).
- The numbered menu renumbers after every action, so multi-number input like "3 1" does different things than you expect. Twice I hired apprentices I didn't want.
- Only one work action per turn. It's not stated where you'd see it: "You already took your personal action this turn."
- Capitalisation: `You spoke for the Free Traders of Ostia. the Ostia Grain Cartel lead.`, with a lowercase "the" starting the sentence.
- Grammar: `The healers of the Tiber Island sanctuary works against...` ("works" should be "work").
- The plague response has no visible cost. `respond hospice` took all my denarii (782), which then triggered missed dues and loyalty losses.
- "You can still pay down debt; type 'end' when you're done" shows every turn once Attention is gone, but there is no paydown item in the menu. You have to know the command from `help`.
- When the last Attention is spent the turn ends by itself, so an extra `end` skips a whole turn. I lost 3–4 turns this way. Also, turn 72 went to 74 while I only did one action.
- The jump briefing suggests `endow circle <denarii>`, but endowing needs 50% control: "You hold 33% of the Circle; you need 50% to direct it." The briefing should say so or not suggest it.
- At the second departure, the briefing offers `paydown`, `exchange`, `deposit` and `bury`, but every one of them answers "'visit <place>', 'learn more', 'jump' to go on, or 'quit'." It's a dead end: 49 aurei lost and the debts unpaid.
- The forges text on arrival says "the water wheel you paid for", but I chose to let the guild lend Tertius the money.
- The "profited from its bad years twice" backlash felt wrong for a player who paid for flood relief. The game never signalled that selling designs or taking the guild loan counted as profiteering.
- The Circle ends up "Drifted — now the Hospice of the Fountain", which is exactly the camp I ordered. Calling that "Drifted" is confusing.
- After the game ends, `log` doesn't work, so you can't re-read the second arrival.

## Rating
**8/10.** The arrival paid off every choice I made in Rome, with the fountain inscription, the promise and the population number, and I really wanted to see the next jump. But the middle of the era was a grind of consult / end / pay dues, and the renumbering menu kept tripping me up.
