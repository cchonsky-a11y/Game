# Playtest report — t2

**Persona:** Roman history enthusiast who reads the flavor text closely and plays for the story more than the score.
**Seed:** 612 (final hash 4a0b82a21973f3ef)
**Length:** about 150 moves; Rome era AD 155 to 173 (113 turns), then a jump of 60 years to AD 233 and a second jump of 35 years to AD 268.

## Key choices
- **Hour one (AD 155):** chose the **fountain** in the Subura. It cost 40 of my 60 scavenged aurei, and I later had to buy them back for the machine.
- **Machine:** assessed it in 155–56. Borrowed the still from the Physicians' Circle. Bought back the 45 missing aurei in AD 160. All 9 repairs were done by late 164. Added the gilded contacts and the lens upgrades in 171–72.
- **Institutions:** joined the **Physicians' Circle** (AD 155) and rose through seniority and inventions: 10% (159), senior physician (160), voice at 25% (165), second to the archiater (166), 41% by 173. I always voted for the Hospice of the Fountain. Joined the **Merchants' Guild** (157) and always voted Free Traders. I declined the guild office about six times. Founded the **School of the Fountain** in late 169. It **failed** in January 171 because I never invested in it.
- **Workshop:** bought a share (156) and hired apprentices. Lost both apprentices in 161 because I couldn't pay their wages. Expanded to the yard (159). Let Tertius **sell the designs** to the guild (163). Let him **borrow from the guild** for the river forge (168).
- **Inventions:** soap (157), distilled wine (159), fever ward (167–68). All three were in the hygiene branch.
- **Rome's choices:** Tiber flood: **relief** (161–62). Parthian levy: **ducked** (162). Galen: **sponsored** (162). Sabinus's grain cartel: **refused, spoke for open prices** (164). Marcus's auction: **funded relief** (169). Invasion scare: waited it out (170). Late grain fleet: **shared** (172).
- **Promise:** Demetria asked me in AD 165 to stay through the sickness. I said **yes** and kept it.
- **Plague:** quarantine rules for Ostia (163), a physician for the district (165), Medicine set to Protect (165). My response was **hospice**. Result: "Contained," 2% of Rome (history says 10%). The Plague of Cyprian (251) was also contained at 2%.
- **Policy:** none. I never had a Governance voice and never advocated.
- **Departure:** AD 173 (Index 151). Last orders: Hospice at the Circle ("real weight") and Free Traders at the Guild ("almost no weight"). Carried 10 aurei, deposited 40 at the bank, buried 37.

## The four questions
1. **What were you choosing between during the Rome era?** Mostly between money and standing. Consulting for the rich paid best, but orders for physicians and the guild built ties. Buying stake, saving for inventions, and saving the scavenged gold for the machine all competed for the same denarii. Attention was the harder limit. Taking an office made my vote count but cost me every turn, and I kept choosing the Circle over the Guild. The dated events were real moral choices: relief versus profit, Galen versus my own Circle, a cartel versus free prices. The biggest choice was when to leave: stay for the plague and keep my promise, or go once the machine was fixed in 164.
2. **What changed in the world you came back to?** The fountain still runs, with AQUA PURA carved on the rim. The Physicians' Circle turned into the Hospice of the Fountain, the camp I voted for, and has a line over the door "For the one who stayed." Rome is bigger (1.42M), with more children and more old people. Medicine is at 217% of history, the plagues hit at 2% instead of 10%, and the healers use soap, spirits and a fever ward. The forge and river works are still running, and the Tertian ship-fitting pattern is made from Ostia to Carthage. The coin has collapsed to 3% silver and wheat costs far more. The guild I backed became the Ostia Grain Cartel. My school is a bathhouse.
3. **Did any of that feel connected to you? Which part?** Yes, strongly. The fountain rim, "For the one who stayed" (my kept promise), and my misspelled name on the roll. "hospes" scratched by the flood line where I paid for boats. Galen's demonstration notice still on the wall of the hall I paid for. The cartel hurt in a way that also felt earned: I had refused the office that would have given my words weight.
4. **Would you jump again to see what happens next?** Yes, absolutely. I want to see whether the Hospice survives the rest of the third-century crisis. I'm also a little sore that I missed walking around AD 233.

## Bugs, dead ends and confusing text
- **The walk-around text doesn't age during the era.** In AD 173, `visit subura` still says "The district fountain runs brown, and a mother pulls her child away from it". I fixed it in 155. `visit forges` still says "the smith you've just paid is clearing space for a second forge… still swearing at his cracked bellows", though by then it was a works on the river.
- **The second jump fires with no confirmation or pause.** Typing `jump` after the first arrival jumped again at once, so I never walked around AD 233. (My piped output cut off the end of the first arrival, but the game offered no "are you sure / walk around first" step.)
- **The gold ledger contradicts itself after the second arrival.** The Discovery beat says "The 87 aurei you couldn't carry, and didn't hide or bank, are long gone". But `learn more` says "deposited 0 (87 back)… left behind 87; you hold 97 aurei now." It's unclear whether I have 97 or lost 87. I did bank 40 and bury 37 before the first jump.
- **The Discovery beat says the Circle keeps "a copy of your charter under glass".** I never chartered the Circle, and I didn't control it.
- **The jump briefing suggests `endow circle <denarii>` and `endow guild <denarii>`.** Both are refused unless I hold 50%.
- **Paying down debt with 0 Attention silently ends the turn,** even though the prompt says "You can still pay down debt; type 'end' when you're done." My next `end` then skipped a whole turn. This happened twice (turns 56 and 103).
- **Committed Attention auto-skipped 4 turns with no warning** when I started the quarantine ("4 turns pass; your Attention was fully committed"). It also skipped a news beat and a year-start.
- **The apprentices left for unpaid wages without appearing in the turn summary.** It was only in `log`.
- **The plague response looks meaningless.** `why plague` shows "about 2%" for quarantine, hospice and none alike. Hospice's cost (750 denarii, 2 Attention) wasn't shown before I chose.
- **Guild camps:** "You spoke for the Free Traders of Ostia. Neither camp leads yet." This came right after earlier turns said "the Free Traders of Ostia lead, as you want," even with my votes at 12–0.
- **Grammar and truncation:**
  - "the Hospice of the Fountain lead, as you want." (lowercase "the"; should probably be "leads" or "is leading")
  - "Your work as senior physician … keep Medicine up" (should be "keeps")
  - "Theon of Alexandria follows their own judgment more"
  - The menu truncates event titles: "A levy for: pay your share…", "Galen comes to: sponsor…", "Tertius wants to: …"
- **Inventions list shows empty rewards:** "the guild:  (if you're a member)".
- **School log causality:** "Without your presence, Theon … Loyalty 70 → 60." I was present in Rome. Then "The School recruits members… because: Without your presence…", which is an odd cause.
- **The Circle stopped gaining seniority at 40% with no explanation** until I read the institutions screen ("it takes a 50% stake to lead it").
- **Forges on arrival:** "the water wheel you paid for". I refused to pay; the guild lent it. The line "The Ostia Grain Cartel: drifted, strength 15" also appears oddly inside the forges description.
- **Walk-around population "when you left" is ambiguous.** In AD 268, "about 1,362,000 when you left" refers to the AD 233 stop, not my departure from the era (~980,000).
- **The guild office offer repeats every January** after being declined, and clutters the menu with a "Decide now" line.

## Rating
**8/10.** The flavor writing, the historical events and the arrival beats made me feel my choices became Rome's history, and I badly want to see what the next jump holds. The middle years are a grind, and several bookkeeping surprises (skipped turns, silent losses) took me out of the story.
