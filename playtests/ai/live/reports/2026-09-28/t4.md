# Playtest report: t4

**Persona:** an economics-minded player who cares about money, prices, trade and markets, and wants to see what economic ideas can do in ancient Rome.
**Seed:** 614
**Length:** about 190 moves. I played the Rome era from AD 155 to late 171, jumped 40 years to AD 212, then jumped again 45 years to AD 257.

## Key choices, with years
- **Hour-one choice (AD 155):** the workshop, not the fountain. I wanted property and income. In January 157 the game told me: "Fever in the district: children who drink from the broken fountain fall sick. You remember choosing the workshop over the fountain." I repaired the fountain myself in late 157 once memberships had made it cheaper.
- **Money:** I paid all 60 aurei back into the machine early (20 in 156, then bought 40 in 157). I did this before the denarius could lose more value.
- **Institutions:**
  - Merchants' Guild of Ostia: joined in 155 and reached 10% by 159. I was quaestor from 162 to 167, then resigned so I could charter the Club. I ended at 28%, which gave me a voice, and set Economy to Protect.
  - Junian faction: joined in 157 for property and archive access. Ended at about 17%.
  - Tiber Island sanctuary: joined in 157. Ended at 22%. I declined its benefactor office about five times.
  - Club of the Aventine: founded in 164 for 5,775 denarii so I could set policy. I chartered it (168), endowed it (1,800 denarii), gave it an audit charter (171), and named Gnaea Seia as successor with orders to back the Reformers.
- **Policy:**
  - Before I had a voice, I argued for sound coinage (155) and free prices (160).
  - Through the Club, I set property secure (164) and taxes light (168).
  - I funded the mint audit (159, Economy +6) and honest weights and free prices at the Forum Boarium (161, Economy +8).
- **Inventions:** double-entry bookkeeping (156, consulting +20% and guild stake), then soap and boiled linen (158).
- **Workshop:**
  - Let Tertius sell my designs to the guild (163). This brought in a lump of gold but lowered order prices.
  - Let the guild lend for the river forge (168).
  - I never expanded the workshop or hired apprentices.
- **Rome's choices:**
  - Tiber flood (161): paid for relief.
  - Parthian levy (162): paid.
  - Galen (162): sponsored him.
  - Sabinus's grain cartel (164): refused and spoke for open prices.
  - Sanctuary wing (160): clinic. I picked this by accident when the menu numbers shifted, but I would have chosen it anyway.
  - Marcus's palace auction (169): paid for relief.
  - Marcomanni invasion scare (170): held firm and bought what others were dumping.
- **Promise:** promised Demetria in 165 that I would stay through the plague, and kept it.
- **Plague:**
  - I drafted quarantine rules for Ostia's docks (166, Medicine +5, Economy −3 from lost trade).
  - The outbreak ended before my "respond quarantine" took effect.
  - Result: 3% dead (history: 10%), labelled "contained". Iulia, an orphaned apprentice physician, appeared as an opening.
- **Jump:** 1 of 3 machine upgrades done. Before leaving I paid Governance debt down to 0, buried 50 aurei, deposited 27 with the bank and carried 10. I left in late AD 171 (the briefing said AD 172). The machine carried me 40 years to AD 212. I then jumped again at once: 45 years to AD 257.

## The four questions
1. **What were you choosing between during the Rome era?** Mostly spending money and Attention on income now or on influence later. Consulting paid, but buying stakes and founding the Club gave me the power to set policy. I also had to choose between sound economics and short-term goodwill: free prices and sound coin cost Governance debt and loyalty, and quarantine cost Ostia trade. Early on it was the workshop against the fountain, and later the Club's strength against the rivals it provoked. In the dated events I was choosing between profit and generosity: flood relief or profiteering, the cartel or open prices, a gold cup or bread.
2. **What changed in the world you came back to?** In AD 212 the Forum Boarium had spilled past its walls, with goods from half the world. The fountain still ran, my workshop was still working, merchants kept double-entry books and healers boiled their linen. The Club still carried my policy line, and the Index was 174 (Economy 221). By AD 257 the Junians had been captured by the landlords and the sanctuary had drifted to the Shrine, but the Club was still thriving and the Economy was nearly three times history. Prices kept rising and the coin kept losing silver, just more slowly than in real history.
3. **Did any of that feel connected to you? Which part?** Yes, strongly. The carved line "For the one who stayed" (my promise to Demetria), the Club's "Begun by a stranger from the East", the clinic wing I paid for with its queue for boiled linen, the "hospes" scratched by the flood line, and the fountain I repaired late. The economic mark felt connected too: the Curia still quoting my four stances.
4. **Would you jump again to see what happens next?** Yes. I especially want to know whether the Club survives the 3rd-century crisis and whether a sound coin could hold back AD 268.

## Bugs, dead ends and confusing text
- **Stale market text:** in AD 160 `visit market` still said "Nobody here has ever seen a zipper, and several people stare… You have no denarii. You have gold." By then I had 500 denarii, no aurei, and had lived there for five years.
- **Empty payoff text** in the invention list: "the guild:  (if you're a member)" (wheelbarrow, collar and trip hammer).
- **Advocacy effect not shown:** `why policy` says Austrian stances "grow the Economy 1 a year each", but `why economy` never lists any growth from the stances I advocated. Only projects and dues appear.
- **Policy cost shown as 1 Attention, charged as 2:** `why policy` says "policy <issue> <stance>, 1 Attention", but setting property and taxes cost 2 Attention (help also says 2).
- **Gold and denarii mixed up:** charter said "The Club is chartered (30 gold, 3 Attention)… with an endowment of 60+ gold". Everything else is priced in denarii. Actual cost: about 768 denarii.
- **Unfair "profiteer" judgment:** "The Subura has seen you profit from its bad years twice." It was triggered by letting the guild lend for the river forge. I had never profiteered: I paid flood relief and the levy and refused the cartel.
- **Wrong numbers and odd causes in the Club events:**
  - The rival attack was printed twice, both lines saying "Strength 24 → 21", while strength actually went 28 → 21.
  - `why club` gives odd causes: "The Club recruits members… because: Without your presence, Titus Flavius Rufus follows their own judgment more."
- **Output from another game in my session:** twice (after `log 15` and after `why governance`), text appeared that didn't match my game. There was a menu with "attend faction" and "attend bank" (I belonged to neither), and log lines about AD 169 with "prices now 23.2% above AD 155" while my game was in AD 168 at +13.3%.
- **Plague response lapsed at once:** I got "► Waiting: respond quarantine / respond none", then the outbreak resolved at the end of that same turn. Next turn: "There is no outbreak to respond to." Other decisions give 3 turns.
- **Response cost hidden:** the plague response cost (500 denarii) wasn't shown until I tried it.
- **Turns end automatically, inconsistently:** a turn ends by itself when Attention runs out and there is no debt. With any debt it waits for `end`. This made me waste turns twice.
- **Sanctuary office offered repeatedly:** after I declined it, the benefactor office kept coming back every few turns.
- **Workshop yard I never rented:** at both arrivals `visit forges` says "Behind it the yard you rented is still a yard". I never expanded the workshop (the guild lent for a river forge instead).
- **The coin text contradicts itself:**
  - Recognition/Wrongness says the denarius "still rings true… the one thing Rome never cheapened".
  - The changers in AD 212 report 67% silver (76% when I left) and the aureus up from 28.9 to 35.6.
  - The briefing promised "Rome's mint won't cut the silver" while the Club stands. It did stand, and the silver still fell to 42% by AD 257.
  - At the second arrival the changers said "51% when you left", but at the first arrival (AD 212, which is when I left) they said 67%.
- **Second-jump deposit refused:** the second-jump briefing said "48 aurei would be left behind and lost (deposit <n> or bury <n>…)". `deposit 48` then answered "'visit <place>', 'learn more', 'jump' to go on, or 'quit'", and the 48 aurei were lost.
- **Gold line in learn more:** after the second jump it says "deposited 0 (48 back)".
- **No second confirmation on the second jump:** the first jump needed `jump` twice. After the refused deposit, a single `jump` left immediately.
- **Grammar:** "The healers of the Tiber Island sanctuary … is still there and still itself"; "Your work as magister of the Club keep Governance up".
- **"Drifted" in the right direction:** "The Merchants' Guild of Ostia: Drifted — now the Free Traders of Ostia". That is the camp I backed, so it's unclear whether "drifted" is good or bad here.
- **Leaving date:** the briefing said I would leave "AD 172", but it was November AD 171.

## Rating: 8/10
The economic levers (mint audit, the free market, sound coin through my own club) visibly shaped the Rome I came back to, and the arrival writing made it personal. The grindy Attention-starved middle years and a few contradictory coin numbers keep it from a 9.
