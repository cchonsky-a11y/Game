# Playtest report: t6

**Persona:** A self-interested opportunist. I played the inventor as someone out to get rich and get home, and I took the profitable option whenever the game offered one.
**Seed:** 616
**Length:** about 200 commands. Rome era AD 155 to 173 (110 turns), then two jumps: AD 173 → 228 → 268.

## Key choices (with years)
- **Hour one (AD 155):** I took the **workshop** over the fountain because a share of a business meant income. By AD 157, children were getting fever from the broken fountain ("You remember choosing the workshop over the fountain"). The foul water added +10 to the plague hazard. I repaired the fountain late, in AD 166, during the plague warnings, for about 875 denarii.
- **Money engine:** Consulting was by far the best pay. I invented double-entry bookkeeping (AD 156, +20% consulting and a guild stake) and bills of exchange (AD 161, +100/yr and a 10% bank stake). The warehouses project (AD 157) was a poor return: 84/yr on 2,250 denarii.
- **Institutions:** I joined the Merchants' Guild (AD 155), which became my power base. I went quaestor → decurio → quinquennalis (AD 165), reached 51% control by AD 169 and ended at 56%. I joined the Caecilian faction (AD 156) and bought up to a 25% voice in AD 172. I declined its amicus office about six times because Attention was too scarce. I joined the Tiber Island sanctuary (AD 159) mainly because it made a machine repair free. I joined the banking house (AD 160, 5% deposit) and reached 23%.
- **Camps:** I voted the Ostia Grain Cartel at the guild and the Caecilian Houses at the faction every time. My last orders backed the Cartel and named Sabinus's nephew, Gaius Sabinus Magnus, as successor. I backed the Creditors at the bank and the Infirmary at the sanctuary.
- **Rome's choices (the opportunist ledger):**
  - Backed Varro's praetor (158).
  - **Profited** from the Tiber flood (161). I bought soaked grain, dried it and sold it dear, for about +1,650.
  - Ducked the Parthian levy (162).
  - Sponsored Galen (162), as a hedge against the plague.
  - Declined the sanctuary's new wing (160).
  - Let Tertius **sell the designs** to the guild (163). This triggered "The Subura has seen you profit from its bad years twice. Children throw mud at your door."
  - **Signed** the grain price-fixing arrangement (164).
  - Let Tertius borrow from the guild for the river forge (168). I was the guild's head, so it cost me nothing.
  - The treasury loan request lapsed without my input (167; see bugs).
  - Stayed out of the palace auction (169) because I couldn't afford the gold cup.
  - **Held** and bought panic stock during the invasion scare (170).
  - **Hoarded** grain when the fleet was late (172).
- **Policy (AD 172):** Once I had the faction voice I set taxes light and property secure. Both served my own interest.
- **Workshop:** I barely touched it and never took an order. It ended as "works on the river" because the guild paid for the river forge.
- **Promise:** I said **yes** to Demetria (AD 165) and stayed through the plague, which was free for me since I was staying anyway.
- **Plague (AD 166):** I chose **quarantine**, which cut the toll from about 7.1% to 5.4%. The outbreak killed about 54 thousand people (5% of Rome). I kept my promise.
- **Machine:** It was fully repaired by AD 165 and I added all 3 upgrades by AD 169 (range 50–60).
- **Jump:** I left in AD 173. I changed about 5,400 denarii into 161 aurei, deposited 100 with the (venal) bank, buried 50 and carried 10. I jumped 55 years to AD 228, then **accidentally** jumped again at once, 40 years to AD 268 (see bugs).

## The four questions
1. **What were you choosing between during the Rome era?** Mostly between my own money and Rome's good. Take the profit on the flood, the grain fix and the hoard, or pay for relief. Spend scarce Attention on earning (consulting), climbing offices, or the machine. Late in the era it was gold I could carry across time versus endowing or paying down debt for the Rome I'd leave. I also chose between the Cartel and the Free Traders, and between quarantine (costly to my own guild) and doing nothing.
2. **What changed in the world you came back to?** Rome is far richer (Economy index 266) and trade reaches everywhere. Merchants keep double-entry books and pass bills of exchange. My guild is now a thriving grain cartel run by the nephew I picked. The faction and the bank were captured by hereditary families and state creditors. The sanctuary went rogue into a charm-selling shrine hostile to physicians. The coin is nearly worthless, the fountain runs clean, the Subura is healthier, and Galen's nerve drawings are taught everywhere.
3. **Did any of that feel connected to you? Which part?** Yes, strongly. The Cartel is the clearest: I voted for it every meeting, signed the price-fixing and named the successor, and my name is on its roll as a lucky charm. The "stayer's supper" came from my promise to Demetria. The Tertian ship fittings exist because I let Tertius sell my designs. The bookkeeping and bills are my inventions in use. The rogue sanctuary felt like the price of ignoring it.
4. **Would you jump again to see what happens next?** Yes. I'd especially like to see the AD 228 arrival I skipped by accident, and whether the Cartel eats Rome.

## Bugs, dead ends, confusing text
- **Double jump with no arrival shown (major).** At the briefing I typed `jump` (go) and then `jump` again. The second one fired immediately from the first arrival, so AD 228 flew past and I landed in AD 268. A second jump should need its own confirmation, or at least show the arrival and require one keypress first. The final screen then labels AD 228 as "Departure", which I never saw.
- **Gold text contradicts itself.** Arrival: "The 210 aurei you couldn't carry, and didn't hide or bank, are long gone." Learn more: "carried 10, deposited 0 (210 back), buried 0 (0 recovered), left behind 210; you hold 220 aurei now." Do I hold 220 or were 210 lost? I'm guessing the bank and the jar paid out at the first arrival and I then jumped without re-banking, but the text doesn't make that clear.
- **A decision lapsed during auto-advance.** Running the lens upgrade with all Attention committed, the game passed about 6 turns on its own ("2 turns pass; your Attention was fully committed" and later silently). "A loan to the treasury" (decide vouch/refuse) opened and lapsed to "refuse" without ever being shown. It cost me loyalty 47→39 with Crispus. Open prompts should stop the auto-pass.
- **The turn-end rule confused me.** Sometimes using the last Attention ends the turn ("No Attention left: the turn ends"). Sometimes it doesn't because debt could be paid down ("You can still pay down debt; type 'end'"). When I typed `end` after an action that had already ended the turn, I skipped a whole turn. That happened several times (e.g., turn 22→24, 78→80, 80→82).
- **Placeholder names when you are the head.** "Tertius wants a forge on the river: you of the Guild thinks better of you. Loyalty 91 → 95." and "you gives you a larger say in the Guild: 48%." The status line also reads "The Guild (you)".
- **Contradictory guild description.** "The Merchants' Guild of Ostia still meets at the harbor, still arguing for open markets and honest coin. Its head is Gaius Sabinus Magnus, Master of the Cartel." A price-fixing cartel arguing for open markets?
- `attend guild freetraders` reported "the Ostia Grain Cartel lead, as you want", although I had just voted Free Traders (my typo, but the message says "as you want").
- `buy bank` without a percent fails, even though the menu said "join bank (1,688 denarii)". The error was clear, but I only saw it after planning the turn around it.
- `inventions` payoff text has empty clauses: "the guild:  (if you're a member)".
- Multi-turn projects and repairs quietly reserve Attention every turn. The costs are listed as "2 Attention a turn for N turns", but it didn't sink in until I was stuck at 1/4 for a year. Offices add more drain on top.
- Faction office was re-offered almost every January after I declined it. That got a bit naggy.
- The warehouses (2,250 for 84/yr) and workshop orders (about 200 for 2 Attention, versus 400+ for crafting) felt like traps for a money-focused player. Consulting dominates.

## Rating: 7/10
Playing a profiteer and watching Rome remember me, through mud on my door, a lucky-charm name on the Cartel's roll, captured institutions and a supper for "the one who stayed", was genuinely compelling. But the accidental double jump robbed me of the first arrival, and the Attention bookkeeping in the middle years felt like chores.
