# Playtest report: t7

**Persona:** An impatient player who wants to fix the time machine and leave as fast as possible, and cares about Rome only where it helps with that.
**Seed:** 617
**Length:** about 60 moves. I left in turn 26 (March AD 159), four years into a twenty-year era.

## Key choices
- **Hour one (AD 155):** I chose the **workshop**, because it paid an income and the fountain didn't. It cost 40 of my 60 aurei. I assessed the machine on turn 1.
- **Machine gold:** On turn 6 (AD 155) I bought the missing 40 aurei with consulting money (1,025 denarii) and put all 60 back. That requirement took almost no effort.
- **Institutions:** In AD 156 I joined the **Tiber Island sanctuary** at 1%, only because membership made the coolant repair free. Seniority took me to 4% by AD 159. I voted once for the Infirmary camp (AD 158) and left it last orders to back the Infirmary. The game said these carried "almost no weight". I joined nothing else.
- **Policy:** None. I never advocated for anything.
- **Workshop:** I owned a share (+20 denarii a turn). I took no orders, hired no apprentices and never expanded it.
- **Rome's choices / decision events:** None came up before I left. I never met Demetria and never got the promise.
- **Repairs:** I ran all three systems in parallel, AD 156–159. 9/9 steps were done by March AD 159. I bought no upgrades.
- **Fountain:** I funded it late (AD 158–159) with money I would otherwise have lost, partly out of guilt after the fever event.
- **Before the jump:** I paid down Medicine debt (79 den.), changed my denarii to 11 aurei, buried 1 and carried 10.
- **Plague:** I left before any warning. It struck in AD 166 with no response: "severe, about 82 thousand dead".
- **Jumps:** First jump AD 159 → AD 189 (30 years). Second jump AD 189 → AD 219 (30 years).

## The four questions
1. **What were you choosing between during the Rome era?** At the start, the fountain for the neighbours or the workshop for my own income. After that it was mostly speed against cost: which kind of work gave the most denarii per Attention, and whether a membership was worth buying to make a repair step free. Late on I chose between spending my leftover money on the fountain and just letting it vanish. I never faced the bigger choices (factions, policy, the plague) because I left before they came.
2. **What changed in the world you came back to?** Very little. My workshop had become a stable and later a ruin. The fountain I fixed still ran in 189 but was dry by 219. Prices rose and the coin lost silver, but that seems to be history anyway. The plague killed about 82,000 with no response. The Index sat at 97–98, slightly below real history.
3. **Did any of that feel connected to you? Which part?** Yes, a bit: the workshop turned into a stable, and the fountain was still running on my first return. The strongest moment was "about 82 thousand dead... You aren't in any of them." It made me feel I had run away. The game also told me plainly that no one remembered me, which matched how I played.
4. **Would you jump again to see what happens next?** I did jump once more, mostly because my persona wants to get home. After the second arrival I'd only jump again if I could play another era. Watching a Rome I didn't shape drift along history isn't much of a pull. The game did make me curious about what a player who stayed would have seen.

## Bugs, dead ends and confusing text
- **Menu labels that aren't commands:** the menu shows `[12] join sanctuary (310 denarii)`, but typing `join sanctuary` returns "Unknown command. Type 'help'." Only the number or `buy sanctuary` works.
- **Workshop contradictions:**
  - Recognition in AD 189 says "the smith's workshop you funded by its soot-black wall. It is a stable now." `visit forges` in the same arrival says it "has shrunk to a single cold forge."
  - In AD 219 Recognition says "the old forge has fallen in; weeds grow through the hearth", but `visit forges` repeats "shrunk to a single cold forge."
- **Grammar:**
  - "It is still there: 1 aurei, green with damp" and "The 1 aurei you couldn't carry" should say "1 aureus". The jump screen gets it right ("1 aureus would be left behind").
  - "You spoke for the Infirmary of the Island. the Keepers of the Island Shrine lead." has a lowercase "the" starting a sentence.
- **Burying before the second jump:** the second jump screen offers "deposit <n> or bury <n>" for the extra aureus, but `bury 1` after arrival only answered "'visit <place>', 'learn more', 'jump' to go on, or 'quit'." I lost the aureus as a result. The prep line also lists paydown/endow/exchange, which can't be used there.
- **Debt shown only when I can't act:** the second jump screen reveals large debts (Medicine 27, Governance 35.9, Economy 16.6) with paydown costs in the thousands of denarii. I had 0 denarii and no actions, so the information is useless and a little alarming.
- **Demetria:** she appears in the second arrival's Personal echo ("Demetria's list of those who stayed in 166"), but I never met her. The promise never triggered because I left in 159.
- **Fountain wording:** "The fountain you repaired later still runs" and "The fountain you repaired late is dry again" read oddly.
- **Too easy to leave:**
  - Restoring the machine's gold was trivial: buy the 40 aurei on turn 6 with three turns of consulting.
  - All 9 repairs can run in parallel, and one Sanctuary membership made several steps free.
  - I finished in 4 years, before the plague warnings, the promise or any decision event. A player like me skips most of the game's content.
- **Arrival in AD 189 during the recurrence:** the first arrival lands in the year of the 189 plague, which killed 7% of Rome (history had 3%). Only `learn more` mentions it; the arrival beats and the Subura visit don't show a city in the middle of an epidemic.
- **Fountain value:** the fountain was only +1 Medicine for 900 denarii, which felt disproportionately small.

## Rating
**5/10.** The arrival writing hit hard for a player who fled: "You aren't in any of them" and the workshop turned into a stable both landed. But I could leave so quickly that I saw almost none of the game, and the returned Rome was "the Rome of the history books", so there was little to come back for.
