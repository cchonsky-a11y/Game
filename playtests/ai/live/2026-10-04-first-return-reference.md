## Arrival: AD 199 (40 years)

**Recognition.** You climb out of the machine in AD 199. The Tiber smells the same. You find the district fountain you repaired by the shape of the street, but the basin is cracked and dry, full of leaves. Of the smith you didn't choose, there is no trace.

**Wrongness.** Something is wrong, and it takes you a while to name it: beyond the few things you recognize, this is Rome almost exactly as the history books describe AD 199. The coins are lighter and paler than you remember, barely half silver now. Prices have climbed with every cut, and soldiers grumble that their pay buys less each year.

**Personal echo.** You left before the first rumors came from the East. In the city's records, the pestilence of 166 fills page after page: about 83 thousand dead in Rome. You aren't in any of them. In a family archive at Ostia a marriage contract survives with seven seals. One is a device nobody can identify: a little wheel inside a square. The bakery on the Clivus has been in one family for three generations, the baker's son Livia chose for her daughter. A ribbon hangs over the oven, faded from green to grey, and nobody has dared take it down.

**Discovery.** You ask at the guild halls and the Curia whether anyone remembers you. No one does: you never held enough of anything in Rome to leave your mark on it. In a few shops on the Clivus and at Ostia the rules on the walls are copies of one bronze bar. The men there say a thing is cut to the foot and mean that one. Three shops still cut seats to a shared set of gauges, and their pumps trade parts. In a few cellars under the Aventine the pumps still have seats cut true, refitted by men who learned it in Cassianus's cellar. Nowhere else. The Circle's dining book still lists you twice as Serenus's guest, under a name spelled two different ways.
