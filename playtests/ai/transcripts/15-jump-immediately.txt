THE BUTTERFLY EFFECT — P0 Butterfly Test
========================================
Rome, AD 155. Your time machine failed and left you here. You have a little gold, what you know,
and a small circle of people willing to listen. Something is coming from the East in a few years.
When you're ready, the machine can carry you 250 years forward. What you leave behind will go on without you.

This prototype covers one era, one jump, and your arrival. When you leave Rome, you'll see what became of it, and the test ends there.

First, a choice. You can afford only one:
  choose fountain  — The Subura's fountain runs brown. Clear the lead pipe and reline the basin.
  choose workshop  — Equip the smith by the Porta Trigemina with a second forge and paid apprentices.
Type 'help' for commands.

== Turn 1 · Ianuarius, AD 155 ==  Gold 60 (+14.5/turn)   Attention 4/4   Index 100
  Medicine      50  expect   50  Maintain    debt     0 Stable
  Governance    55  expect   55  Maintain    debt     0 Stable
  Economy       55  expect   55  Maintain    debt     0 Stable
  ► Waiting: choose fountain or choose workshop (before the end of turn 2).

> jump
You will leave AD 155 for AD 405. You can't come back.
What you leave behind (60 gold in hand):
  • No institution will look after Rome while you're away.
  • The 60 gold in your hands stays behind and is lost unless you spend it, pay down debt or endow an institution.
Prepare: paydown <domain> <points> · endow <inst> <gold|all> · audit <inst>. Type 'jump' again to go, or anything else to stay.

> jump

The machine shudders. Decades pass in the dark...

— Recognition —
You climb out of the machine in AD 405. The Tiber smells the same. You pass the corner where the
smith worked and the fountain ran foul. Neither is there. You remember letting both chances pass.

— Wrongness —
Something is wrong, and it takes you a while to name it: beyond the few things you recognize, this is
Rome almost exactly as the history books describe AD 405.

— Personal echo —
You left before the first rumors came from the East. In the city's records, the pestilence of 162
fills page after page: about 110 thousand dead in Rome. You aren't in any of them.

— Discovery —
You ask after Demetria of Pergamon and Senator Varro. No one has heard either name. Nothing you built
outlasted you.

Type 'learn more' for the Index and what became of your institutions, or 'quit'.
