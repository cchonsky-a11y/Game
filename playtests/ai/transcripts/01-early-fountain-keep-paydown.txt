THE BUTTERFLY EFFECT

The machine stops screaming before you do.

You sit strapped in, sweating, your hands shaking on the armrests. It was a systems test. Nothing was
supposed to go anywhere. The panels are dark and the controls are dead.

You open the hatch expecting the concrete floor of the test bay. You get dirt. A mule cart rattles
past close enough to touch, and the driver shouts at you in Latin. You understand him.

Thank God I took that in college.

Ancient Rome. Not ruins. Alive.

The machine is dead weight. Its contacts and couplings are gold, and gold you can carry: you pry out
60 aurei worth of it. Every piece will have to go back before the machine could ever run again.
Rome pays in silver denarii. Gold has to be changed at the money changers (exchange <n> aurei).

Two things on this street could use your money, and you can afford only one:
  choose fountain  — The Subura's fountain runs brown. Clear the lead pipe and reline the basin.
  choose workshop  — Pay for a second forge and paid apprentices at the smith's by the Porta Trigemina, in exchange for a share of the business.
Your ears are still ringing. Before anything else you could walk and get your bearings: visit market, changers, forges, curia or subura (no Attention).
Type 'help' for commands.

The machine shudders. Decades pass in the dark... It carries you 35 years.

— Recognition —
You climb out of the machine in AD 195. The Tiber smells the same. In the Subura, the district
fountain you repaired still runs: the basin is worn smooth, and someone has cut two words into its
rim: AQUA PURA. Of the smith you didn't choose, there is no trace.

— Wrongness —
Something is wrong, and it takes you a moment to see it: children, everywhere. More than any Roman
street you remember, and fewer small graves along the Via Appia. The coins are lighter and paler than
you remember, barely half silver now. Prices have climbed with every cut, and soldiers grumble that
their pay buys less each year.

— Personal echo —
You left before the first rumors came from the East. In the city's records, the pestilence of 166
fills page after page: about 76 thousand dead in Rome. You aren't in any of them. In the Circle's
library a casebook in a careful hand sits chained to the shelf: Serenus's fevers, set side by side,
with notes in three later hands arguing with him.

— Discovery —
You ask at the guild halls and the Curia whether anyone remembers you. No one does: you never held
enough of anything in Rome to leave your mark on it. In your purse: the 7 aurei you carried, as good
as the day you left.

You can walk around Rome: visit market, changers, forges, curia or subura (no Attention).
